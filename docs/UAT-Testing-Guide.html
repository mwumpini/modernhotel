Ghana Tax Stack — Plain English Explanation

What each levy means · How they stack · What to expect on guest bills

Ghana's hotel industry is governed by the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA). Every guest bill carries four levies that stack on top of each other in a specific order. Here is what each one means:

NHIL
2.5%
National Health Insurance Levy. Funds Ghana's national health system. Applied on the room/service cost before VAT.
GETFund
2.5%
Ghana Education Trust Fund. Funds education infrastructure. Applied on the room/service cost before VAT.
VAT
20%
Value Added Tax. The main consumption tax. Applied on (room cost + NHIL + GETFund) — it stacks on top of the levies.
Tourism Levy
1%
Tourism Development Levy. Funds Ghana Tourism Authority. Applied on room/accommodation charges only, not on food.
WHT
5%
Withholding Tax. Deducted from payments to suppliers for services. Does NOT appear on guest bills.
How the Numbers Stack — Worked Example

If a guest books a room for GHS 1,000 per night, here is how their total bill is calculated:

── Room rate (tax-exclusive) ──────────────────────
Room cost GHS 1,000.00

── Step 1: Apply NHIL & GETFund on room cost ──────
NHIL (2.5%) GHS 25.00
GETFund (2.5%) GHS 25.00
Sub-total after levies GHS 1,050.00

── Step 2: Apply VAT on (room + NHIL + GETFund) ─────
VAT 20% × 1,050.00 GHS 210.00

── Step 3: Tourism Levy on original room cost ───────
Tourism (1%) × 1,000 GHS 10.00

TOTAL GUEST BILL GHS 1,245.00
Effective rate: 24.5% on top of room cost
Key Points to Remember

1. NHIL and GETFund are non-creditable — the hotel cannot recover them on supplier purchases.
2. VAT is creditable — the hotel can offset input VAT paid to suppliers against output VAT collected from guests.
3. The Tourism Levy applies to room charges only, not to restaurant food or spa services.
4. WHT (5%) is deducted only when the hotel pays service contractors/suppliers — guests never see it.

Understanding PAYE Calculations (GRA 2024)

Here is how Ghana PAYE (Pay As You Earn income tax) is calculated on a monthly salary. The tax is progressive — different rates apply to different bands:

Monthly Income BandAnnual EquivalentRate
First GHS 365GHS 4,380 / yr0% — Personal Relief
Next GHS 110 (up to GHS 475)GHS 1,320 / yr5%
Next GHS 130 (up to GHS 605)GHS 1,560 / yr10%
Next GHS 3,000 (up to GHS 3,605)GHS 36,000 / yr17.5%
Next GHS 16,395 (up to GHS 20,000)GHS 196,740 / yr25%
Remainder above GHS 20,000Above GHS 240,000 / yr30%
